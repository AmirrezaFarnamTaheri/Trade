## Findings that can change decisions

P1 means the issue can invalidate the indicated research, accounting or operational claim. P2 means the result needs a qualification or has a narrower failure mode. Scope each rerun to the dependency named below. Preserve the old artifacts and link corrected versions to them.

### 1. P1 · A next-open fill depends on facts available at the end of that bar

`resolve_bar_prices` prices the fill at the next bar's open and decides tradability using that same bar's `entry_eligible`. Native bars derive eligibility from total volume. Aggregated bars also use coverage and the fraction of sub-bars with trades. Those facts mature after the proposed fill. I called the actual helper twice with the same history and opening price of 100. Changing the following bar's volume from 0 to 10 changed the tradable set from empty to BTC. The earlier slow experiment has its own copy of this test, and the v2 schedule generator and GRU dataset builder also filter on the following bar. This affects at least the first experiment and the Bellman, BearMajors, CTA and four-quadrant study family. The design's demand for a positive-volume execution bar needs a precise fill-time rule.

**Repair and test.** Emit the intent from information available at decision time. If a timestamped executable quote or trade is needed to establish liquidity, fill only when it arrives, at its observed terms and size. Historical OHLC-only runs should carry an explicit fill bound. Perturb all subsequent trades, highs, lows and volumes while holding the tape through the decision instant fixed. Demand identical earlier orders, fills and NAV, and verify that a deliberately future-dependent implementation fails. ### 2. P1 · The prospective journals can record a signal before its bar closes

The stitcher accepts an hour with 45 minute records, flags it incomplete below 55, but sets it entry-eligible. Two such hours become a “complete,” eligible 2h bar because the next level counts rows. I reproduced both transitions. Each live scorer then takes the last bar of each symbol independently. It does not require that bar to be closed as of the recording time or synchronized to a common decision instant. A fast symbol can advance the slot while another contributes yesterday's signal. The keyed candle reader has a latest-row limit but no as-of cutoff.

**Observed locally.** In the operational JSONL files at inspection, `signal_2h_bar > as_of` for **6 of 7 v1 rows and 10 of 10 v2 rows**, by up to **64.7 and 99.4 minutes** respectively. This establishes a future bar label. It does not establish that each row traded or that unavailable future trades were received. A partial live bar and its later finalized history can produce different decisions under the same slot key.

**Repair and test.** Cut each read at one recorded watermark, propagate child completeness and quality upward, and require symbol-level freshness at the selected phase boundary. Save the last consumed event and decision IDs. Run the journal at minute 44, 45, 55, 59 and after close, with a missing middle minute, a stalled symbol and late corrections. Replaying the original tape must yield the same decision identity as the recorder. Flag affected old records and start a separately versioned prospective cohort. ### 3. P1 · The forward NAV can gain before the recorded decision and lose history as the database grows

The scorer keeps the last snapshot on each UTC date and applies those weights to the entire interval between daily candle closes keyed by availability date. An allocation registered at 23:00 can be credited with a move since the start of that day. Intermediate positions, actual entries/exits, fees, spread, borrow and cash carry never enter the return calculation. Unmatched holdings silently contribute zero. Only the latest 120 candles per symbol are loaded regardless of the journal's date span.

**Reproduction.** A temporary SQLite database and a fixed two-record journal returned `TRACKING`, NAV **1,100**, on two daily closes of 100 and 110. Appending 121 later candles without changing a single journal byte returned `TRACKING`, NAV **1,000**. This can corrupt longitudinal comparisons and the leverage governor that reads them.

**Repair and test.** Make decisions, modeled fills, fees, lot changes and marks immutable event records. Replay by event time and venue. Store the quote/mark ID and the unpriceable exposure. An old interval must reproduce identically after later inputs arrive. Keep observations, elapsed dates, fully marked return intervals and active independent blocks as separate counters. [Forward scorer](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_forward_journal.py#L395)

### 4. P1 · Clipping returns breaks the claimed e-process under the stated null

The scorer multiplies wealth by a function equivalent on its clipped range to `1 + 2 × clip(return, −0.2, 0.2)`. Consider a mean-zero daily return of +5% with probability 0.9 and −45% with probability 0.1. The expected first multiplier is **1.05**. An e-value for this null must have expectation at most one, including at that first fixed time. This matters even with flawless fills. Remove statistical significance or stopping interpretations from this field until its null, downside bound, predictable bet and costs are stated and justified. A fixed-horizon paired analysis is a simpler interim readout. Add the asymmetric mean-zero distribution as a regression control. [Scoring formula](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_forward_journal.py#L431) · [Ramdas, definitions of e-values and e-processes](https://stat.cmu.edu/~aramdas/icml25/evalue-checklist.pdf)

### 5. P1 · The ledger can destroy equity during a cost-free internal short offset

When an offset trims a slow spot holding and leaves at most 2%, the book credits cash for the sold fraction and deletes the entire lot. A flat-price fixture started with 1,000, bought 500 units at 1, then requested a zero-fee internal offset of 495. The call returned success, the book held no lots and marked **995**. Five units disappeared without any fee or market movement. This specifically affects paths enabling internal offsets. The macro rebalancer explicitly disables that option for its new margin lots.

Keep the dust as marked inventory or perform an explicit full sale with a fill and fee on the whole amount. Assert exact conservation across the 2% cutoff, both sides of the residual-notional cutoff and a partially executable remaining short. Have the operation return executed quantities per leg so callers can distinguish a spot reduction from a newly opened short. [Offset accounting](../../gym-core/src/gym/domain/multi_horizon_book.py#L265)

### 6. P1 · The first experiment uses overlapping fit/evaluation data and charges the veto's fee hurdle twice

The logistic gateway is fitted once on development proposals and then applied back over all dates, including that development period. The promotion branch uses the **full** paired result. The inner calibration gap counts flattened symbol proposals, whereas the label spans five evaluation dates. Five rows can fit inside one date. Calibration diagnostics also score the rows used to fit the sigmoid, and the Brier reference rate comes from the evaluation labels. These are consequential distinctions for a receipt with 6,340 training proposals.

Separately, training payoffs already deduct a round-trip fee. The win/loss magnitudes are learned from these net payoffs. `should_accept` subtracts the round-trip fee again in its decision score. At a 13 bps leg fee this adds **26 bps** to the hurdle, before the explicit 5 bps safety margin. This biases which proposals enter and could change the retirement decision. The current report's full paired interval crosses zero, while the code can assign `RETIRE_INCREMENT` on a lower point estimate alone.

**Repair and test.** Attach an entry and label-available timestamp to each row, split on complete UTC decision groups, purge by the actual interval and generate predictions only after fit and calibration. Reserve separate observations for calibration assessment. Price the payoff gross and deduct incremental costs once, or use net payoff directly. Test a proposal between the single- and double-fee boundaries. Predeclare economic futility before retiring a hypothesis. The fixed arm can remain the operating research control meanwhile. ### 12. P2 · Registration, data freshness and retry behavior need enforceable identities

v2 writes the registration only when the file is absent. Subsequent calls load whichever model is at the path, compute the current code digest and write new rows without requiring either digest or the sleeve weights to match the registration. The CLI can re-freeze the model at the same path. The watchdog checks that a model has the right schema and at least one member, rather than matching the registered hash. | Path | Evidence at the reviewed revision | Change and validation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Journal read volume | The v2 recorder loads up to 15,000 minute and 800 hourly rows per symbol twice, once per phase, for 18 symbols. That is an upper bound of **540,000 minute rows per call** before checking for an existing slot. v1 repeats overlapping work. | Query the latest closed watermark and registration first. Load the shared panel once, fetch the minute tail only after the last finalized native hour, then derive both phases. Measure rows read, allocations and p50/p95 duration on unchanged, one-new-bar and backfill cases. Keep a correction/version key so old prices cannot survive a revision. |
| Latest Wallex quote | On the local market database, SQLite reported `SCAN b` for the journal's symbol join ordered by descending rowid. A query keyed by instrument ID and ordered by availability used the existing `book_top_avail (instrument_id, ts_avail)` index. Candle reads already used their composite index. | Resolve symbol IDs once, cap availability at the watermark, add a deterministic tie-breaker and check quote-age semantics. Compare result IDs on out-of-order insert fixtures. Measure worst case for a quiet symbol before adding any index. |
| Window scoring | With 13,001 two-hour NAV marks and 20 exact-grid weeks, the current scorer took **1.06, 1.06 and 1.51 s**. An indexed lookup of the same 160 endpoints took **0.18–0.28 ms** and reproduced the rounded weekly returns. The latter times cover only lookup. | Sort once and use backward bisection with staleness limits. Measure the complete replacement on irregular grids, including exclusions. The improvement in lookup work is measured; whole-study speedup is unknown. |
| Candidate window construction | The selector repeatedly scans each symbol's full hourly series for every candidate week and scans BTC again. The unified loop reconstructs per-phase timestamp maps for nine configurations × two phases. | Build immutable timestamp indexes once per snapshot and use index ranges for each week. Prepare by-start/by-available/phase mappings once. Profile peak memory as well as wall time. Require equal selected window IDs, OHLC aggregates, fills and NAV on valid exact-grid fixtures. |
| Repeated training and solver work | The latest study builds two GRU score sets, two stopping datasets/models, two schedules and a v2 reference before the overlay sweep. Several adjacent studies rebuild those dependencies. Multiple callers take solver weights without examining convergence. | Persist fold-level predictions keyed by input snapshot, fold cutoff, feature/label/cost configuration, seed and model version. Reuse immutable features, warm-start the optimizer only within an arm, log residual/feasibility/iterations and fall back to existing holdings on failed validation. Verify cached and cold decision tapes match. |
| Statistical inference | Standard-error calculation calls `fmean(boot_means)` inside the loop over every bootstrap mean. The final variance step alone is quadratic in the number of resamples. | Hoist the mean, keep seed and sampling order fixed, compare exact receipts. Invest any saved compute in more *independent* date coverage first. [Bootstrap implementation](../../gym-core/src/gym/domain/statistical_inference.py#L47) |
| Growing operational history | Every v2 call parses the journal for state and calls the full scorer before deduplication. The CLI scores the history again afterward. The heartbeat file is rewritten in tracked evidence. | Maintain an idempotent checkpoint of balances and last slot, periodically rebuild it from the immutable tape, and atomically publish a derived status file. Separate mutable operator state from pinned reviewed research receipts. Accept an interruption between any two writes and show identical results after restart. |

**Benchmark discipline.** Use one pinned SQLite backup or a short explicit read transaction per extraction so a live collector cannot mix generations across separate SELECTs. Record snapshot ID, rows, symbols, UTC range, machine, cold/warm cache, wall time and peak memory. Benchmark the steady-state recorder and full study independently. Compare financial outputs before tuning array layouts, adding multiprocessing or adopting a different database. Concurrent model fitting and the collector should have separate budgets. [SQLite snapshot isolation](https://www.sqlite.org/isolation.html)

## Recommended delivery sequence

1. **First correction PR.** Add the look-ahead, partial-bar, cross-venue fill and disappearing-dust counterexamples. Correct timing and accounting in their owning modules. Generate a single before/after decision tape. Link each affected report to the change.
2. **Forward-account PR.** Introduce versioned registration enforcement, closed-bar watermarks, lots and executable marks. Reproduce a historical journal interval after later candles arrive. Demonstrate replay/live equivalence, restart equivalence and the peak-relative governor. Keep old journals available for forensic reconstruction.
3. **Research-evaluation PR.** Repair the logistic cost convention and splits, per-fold priors, paired bar-source sensitivity, phase-combined window NAV and missing-interval semantics. Make the gate writer consume real test receipts. Publish old/new ranks and attribution of each difference on the same pinned data.
4. **Cost and capacity study.** Rerun native, counterfactual, maker-bound and taker arms with an explicit book per venue, shared size limits and conservative carry. Publish the smallest change in fees, borrow, latency and depth that reverses the decision.
5. **Structural and performance PRs.** Migrate the second consumer of each shared feature, replay and reporting component. Benchmark the recorder's no-new-slot path, indexed/stale quote lookup and largest study on a frozen snapshot. Require event-level parity for behavior-preserving changes.
6. **Next model decision.** Pre-register one simple challenger and one economic endpoint, with a power or futility calculation based on *independent active periods*. Continue the forward paper cohort. A larger universe, RL agent, distributed backtester, additional meta-models, real inventory transfers and funded routing have lower value until the corrected account can explain each unit of P&L. Margin and derivatives here are explicit extensions for paper research beyond the original spot design.

## A menu of experiments after the repairs

These are proposed paper experiments. The cheapest ones reuse existing intents, targets and OHLC history. Execution experiments need the corrected venue-specific event tape. Freeze the question, comparator and decision threshold for the chosen item before opening its results. Ideas drawn from the same tape share evidence. A result from this menu becomes a candidate for a separately registered forward run.

### Extract more value from signals already collected

| Idea | First experiment | What would change the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Give every signal an expiry time.** A slow trend and a two-hour breakout can both say “buy BTC” yet have very different costs of waiting. | On exactly the same intents, compare immediate execution with the next 1, 2 and 4 executable observations. Hold the original exit deadline, venue and size fixed. Count expired opportunities and missed fills in each arm. Estimate a deadline using development dates only. | Give a sleeve its own urgency policy if it improves paired net wealth on the next block. If price movement consumes ignition's edge before the shortest reliably observable delay, retire that execution path. The research precedent for coupling signal decay and trading costs is [Gârleanu and Pedersen](https://docs.lhpedersen.com/DynamicTrading.pdf). |
| **2. Trade disagreement between the slow and fast books.** A fast buy arriving during a slow sell might be a temporary rebound, a regime transition or a chance to save two fees. | Collect all opposite-signed, same-asset intents known by each decision instant. Compare immediate netting, immediate netting plus a brief pause on the residual, and the current policy. Preserve two virtual sleeve records so the strategy that relinquished exposure gets its opportunity cost recorded. | Retain a delay only if savings survive foregone winners and adverse moves on residual orders. If internal netting captures the entire improvement, ship only netting. |
| **3. Force each sleeve to compete against a cheap replica.** The gold overlay might buy stress protection obtainable with a modest static gold weight. | On development dates fit a long-only BTC/XAUT/USDT portfolio with equal starting wealth to approximate each sleeve's daily exposure. Freeze it, charge its rebalance costs, and compare the residual P&L in the next fold and worst market weeks. | Keep the complex sleeve if its residual buys meaningful return or downside protection. If the simpler portfolio matches both within the chosen tolerance, it becomes the challenger. |
| **4. Measure how much of the edge one market event owns.** An 18-symbol rally can look like 18 successful predictions. | Group simultaneous entries into episodes using timestamps and a common market shock rule fixed in development. Replay after removing each episode in turn. Publish the fraction of P&L attributable to the largest three episodes and the number of independent entry days. | A strategy dominated by one episode graduates only if its payoff is explicitly a rare-event thesis with enough independent observations. Compare it with a cheaper rule that simply detects that event. |
| **5. Let the model allocate between urgency and size.** A model may be poor at direction but good at identifying orders that can wait safely. | Freeze the rule-based signal direction. Give the model one choice only: execute the approved slice now or at the next eligible quote before a deadline. Compare with always-now, always-wait and a spread-only rule using identical intended quantities. | Advance only if it reduces implementation shortfall per intended unit, including expiries, while preserving directional exposure through the deadline. |

### Make the portfolio account answer harder financial questions

| Idea | First experiment | What would change the decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **6. Price the option to keep cash.** A marginal slow position can occupy cash that a better ignition trade needs hours later. | Replay a small grid of fixed cash reserves, then one adaptive reserve based solely on past arrival rates of eligible high-edge trades. Log every rejected trade, its requested collateral and the realized value of the trade displaced. Compare at equal drawdown budget against the best reserve chosen on development data. | Keep the adaptive reserve only if avoided displacement pays for idle cash and forgone carry out of sample. The result can justify a larger reserve, a smaller one, or splitting the books. |
| **7. Ask what one extra unit of venue inventory buys.** This extends the roadmap's prefunding proposal into a treasury decision. | Keep combined starting capital constant. Transfer a small amount of *initial paper inventory* from venue A to B across several runs. Freeze strategy signals and impose the same settlement delays. Plot the change in executable terminal wealth per extra USDT at B and repeat under one-venue downtime. | Prefunding has economic value where the marginal reduction in missed trades and fees exceeds carry and concentration costs. A flat curve supports keeping the operationally simpler allocation. |
| **8. Compare gold tokens as a substitution decision.** Two issuers can offer similar directional exposure and different costs of obtaining or unwinding it. | For one pre-existing gold allocation, compare XAUT only, fixed issuer split and switching into the cheaper executable exposure. Use same-time native quotes, held inventory, issuer caps and round-trip switching costs. Record periods when only one instrument can be valued. | Keep switching only if realized savings survive paying to switch back and downside tests under an issuer-specific discount. Gold beta should remain matched across arms. |
| **9. Put an explicit price on leaving a losing trade.** Two strategies with similar marked NAV can have very different emergency exits. | At prespecified timestamps, liquidate copies of the same paper book over several deadlines. Enforce shared depth and venue balances. Report cash actually recovered by each deadline, the residual inventory and shortfall relative to the decision mid. | Choose the portfolio that meets a declared cash-recovery deadline. An attractive marked return can lose its ranking once the account must become spendable. |
| **10. Split TMN performance into separate investment decisions.** A USDT strategy and simply holding dollars can both rise in TMN. | From the same initial TMN balance compare holding TMN, buying and holding USDT, running the strategy in USDT and liquidating back to TMN, and a direct TMN-pair implementation where supported. Use executable conversions on matched timestamps with residual cash shown. | Report the best choice for each objective separately. If the strategy's incremental TMN wealth over the USDT hold disappears after exit conversion, most of the apparent success came from currency exposure. |

| Idea | First experiment | What would change the decision |

| **11. Buy the next data feed on evidence.** More depth, trade prints and faster polling compete for storage, API quota and engineering time. | Start with already recorded high-detail intervals. | **13. See how often a cheaper observation schedule would make the same decision.** | On a frozen tape, shadow fixed-cadence scoring against event-triggered scoring after a closed bar, material quote move or collateral change. Count missed orders, different fills, CPU time and tail latency. | Adopt the cheaper schedule if it meets a declared bound on decision divergence and economic loss. | **14. **My first three picks are 1, 6 and 11.** Signal expiry determines how long the trader can wait. Cash reservation determines which opportunities it can afford when they arrive. Data masking determines which observations justify collecting and processing quickly. Each can yield an actionable negative result. For a larger research bet after native quote coverage accumulates, combine 7 and 12 to ask whether a measurable price lead is still valuable when the account must have capital at the venue that can act on it.
