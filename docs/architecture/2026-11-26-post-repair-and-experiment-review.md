## Findings that change the meaning of a result

### 1. Gold substitution depends on when the order can trade

The strategy calculates a 60 bps relative basis from the two daily closes and exchanges its entire holding at those same prices. I reproduced the receipt with the project's candle reader and then changed only the execution convention. The independent comparison uses 842 shared dates, an initial XAUT holding, the same 32 bps cost per switch and 49 switches in each switching arm. [Gold implementation](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/treasury_liquidity_and_fx_study.py#L158)

| Execution assumption | Total return | Advantage over XAUT hold |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Observe and transact at both signal closes | +138.49% | +92.41 percentage points |
| Observe closes, transact at next bar's opens | +86.85% | +40.77 points |
| Observe closes, transact at next shared closes | +47.29% | +1.21 points |
| Hold XAUT | +46.08% | — |

The receipt was generated at 20:55:41 UTC on November 25. The last included daily mark has a logical availability of 00:00 UTC on November 26. Restricting the data to the 841 dates already closed at generation gives +140.06%, +88.08%, +48.27% and +47.45% respectively. The timing sensitivity survives the cutoff correction. The issuer shock also changes XAUT marks after the trade calculation, so orders continue using unshocked prices during the shock.

There is a reason to keep collecting. In a separate local Nobitex quote sample ending by 21:00 UTC November 25, pairing each XAUT observation with a unique prior PAXG quote within 15 seconds yielded 579 usable pairs and one threshold event. Selling at the bid and buying at the ask implied a +24.17 bps unit-conversion advantage after two illustrative 16 bps fees at the first pair, and +10.99 bps at the next eligible pair. One displayed-price opportunity says little about occurrence rate, available size or round-trip profit. Freeze the trigger, record both executable legs and their sizes, charge residual exposure when one leg misses, and reprice the actual issuer-shock path. A positive next-open backtest still warrants this test.

### 2. Treasury simulations can spend unavailable cash and omit unavailable inventory

**Prefunding.** The loop immediately credits the purchase proceeds back to the spending venue. It drops the returned fill fraction and processes BTC observations followed by ETH observations, so the outage at the end of the array also selects by asset. With 130 USDT on Nobitex, zero on Wallex and four consecutive 125-USDT buys without sales or settlements, the current function reported four orders, 0% missed and 124.20 remaining. Only the first full buy was affordable. The low mean cost on completed orders also omits the opportunity cost of misses. The reported split rankings cannot set venue inventory targets. [Prefunding loop](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/treasury_liquidity_and_fx_study.py#L105)

**Liquidation.** The five-minute, one-hour and one-day runs multiply the last displayed book's quantities by one, four and twenty. They pool bids across venues and drop missing symbols from the denominator. Supplying only a BTC book for a 40% BTC, 30% ETH, 30% XAUT portfolio produced **100% cash recovered and 0% residual** in the 10,000-USDT five-minute case. Sixty percent of the starting inventory had no quoted exit. Even for covered inventory the calculation reports a filled fraction while execution fees appear separately. [Deadline calculation](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/treasury_liquidity_and_fx_study.py#L218)

Implement one chronological venue account for both studies. A fill posts cash, units and fees at its venue. Sales release proceeds at the specified settlement time. Each deadline reports settled cash net of fees, filled units, marked residual units and unpriced units against the **original** portfolio. Repeated snapshots of the same displayed quantity require an explicit replenishment assumption. Test missing symbols, a partial fill, delayed settlement, two competing sleeves, and a venue that goes offline with inventory on it.

### 3. The prospective scorer still answers a different question from the recorded strategy

The scorer takes the last weights on each UTC date and applies them from that date's daily mark to the next mark. A late decision can collect a price rise that occurred earlier that day. Several intraday position changes collapse to one row. A missing endpoint price contributes zero return. Re-scoring looks up marks in the current database with a 5,000-bar cap. Consequently the e-value and the drawdown used by v2 inherit the scorer's timing and data revisions. The quote helper also takes the newest venue observations without the recorder's requested `as_of`, which can attach later routing telemetry to a historical decision. [Forward scorer](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_forward_journal.py#L469) · [Quote lookup](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_forward_journal.py#L168)

Two separate issues reproduce in small cases. Eight daily candles through January 8, queried as of January 4 with a limit of two, returned **zero** bars because SQL selected the newest two before Python applied the cutoff. The append helper serialized two concurrent appends for the same slot but kept **both** rows. The recorder reads seen slots before acquiring the append lock, so two workers can pass that check. A process death can also leave the exclusive lock file behind. Make the availability bound part of the indexed query before limiting. Commit slot identity and event content atomically with a unique key on registration and slot. A conflicting payload for an existing key should be visible to the operator. [Candle reader](../../gym-core/src/gym/adapters/candle_reader.py#L33) · [Journal append](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_forward_journal.py#L289) · [V2 slot check](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/champion_v2_journal.py#L349) · [SQLite conflict handling](https://www.sqlite.org/lang_conflict.html)

At inspection, seven of eight local v1 records and all thirteen v2 records carried a future bar label relative to the row's decision time. The two files contained six and ten distinct engine digests respectively. **Every inspected row predated the first repair commit.** They establish the need for a fresh generation and clean comparison window. They provide no evidence about how a post-repair recorder actually ran. Preserve the legacy rows and label their cohort.

### 4. The clock, cadence and blind studies need different evidence

The recorder and parser now support a venue event timestamp. The local quote database inspected still lacked the `ts_event` column, and the research loader reads receipt time only. The lead/lag table predicts next midpoint moves from the current signed midpoint gap. Its hit calculation filters small gaps while the displayed sample count includes them. Its “swapped” control randomizes signs and the one-step delay varies in wall-clock length. Report counts after every filter, event/receipt coverage by venue, elapsed-time lags, bid-to-ask outcomes and a true venue-label permutation. Preserve unknown exchange times explicitly. [Quote recorder](../../gym-core/scripts/record_multi_venue_quotes.py) · [Matched-book reader](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/treasury_liquidity_and_fx_study.py#L32) · [Lead/lag logic](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/venue_clocks_cadence_and_breakeven_study.py#L45)

Cadence retained 266 of 267 triggers using 7,144 versus 13,003 eligible evaluations. The reported **45.1%** is a reduction in the evaluation counter. The loop still scans the bars and computes the breakout, and the study collects no process timing. The documented volume-spike trigger is also absent from the event condition. Benchmark two genuinely different scheduler paths on the same tape, recording CPU seconds, p95 decision latency, order/fee differences and the result of the single missed trigger. [Cadence code](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/venue_clocks_cadence_and_breakeven_study.py#L89) · [Python process CPU clock](https://docs.python.org/3/library/time.html#time.process_time)

All four blind arms are marked `PENDING_PROSPECTIVE_WINDOW`. Their identity mapping is plaintext in Git, and the generation procedure uses a public deterministic salt. A reviewer can recover identities before committing a verdict. Preserve this run as a dry run. Create a new generation with concealed assignment, commit its digest, lock predictions and the blinded verdict, then reveal the mapping. Declare the minimum active blocks, comparison margin and stopping rule in advance. [Manifest generator](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/experiments/venue_clocks_cadence_and_breakeven_study.py#L148)

Three further experiments become attractive once the first two deliverables work:

- **Price the second leg of a switch.** Hold the gold allocation fixed and compare immediate two-leg execution with bounded partial execution and abstention when one leg disappears. Track residual issuer exposure in token units and the cash cost of restoring target exposure. This tests whether the apparent basis compensates for the leg that fails.
- **Measure the value of one more unit of liquidity.** Add one marginal unit of venue cash or displayed depth on a frozen outage tape. Rank the extra completed opportunities by net portfolio P&L, including subsequent opportunities displaced by their use of capital. This produces a treasury allocation curve and a defensible ceiling on feed or funding spend.
- **Find the smallest failure that changes the chosen policy.** For each contender, find the shortest quote outage, smallest extra cost, or fewest top episodes removed that makes the simple comparator preferable. Publish the crossover alongside how often disturbances of that magnitude have actually occurred. It gives operations an economically grounded abstention rule.
