# Further design decisions for TradingGYM

Companion to the [post-repair review](2026-11-26-post-repair-and-experiment-review.md) and [rare-signal design](rare-micro-signal-research-design.md), reviewed against the plans and source layout at `113528c`. These are proposed paper experiments and extensions. Presence of a related module does not establish that its current implementation has a defect.

## Three experiments with the highest decision value

### 1. Bound a decision when the tape cannot tell you what happened

Suppose a long enters at 100 with a stop at 98 and a target at 104. The next bar opens at 100, trades down to 97 and up to 105, then closes at 101. Both exits are compatible with the observation. The ambiguity is financial: a strategy with tighter exits encounters it more often, so applying one common optimistic convention can systematically favor one arm.

For each contender, replay jointly feasible event orderings for both policies on the same ambiguous bars. Respect gaps, available inventory, observable bid/ask and shared price paths. Start with stop-first and target-first cases as sensitivity scenarios. Call their range a bound only if it covers all admissible outcomes under the stated fill model. Publish the fraction of P&L and the sign of the paired advantage that depend on unresolved events. If ranking reverses, collect finer data on those events. If it survives plausible adverse ordering, move on. This focuses capture money on a specific decision.

### 2. Measure the harm caused by an otherwise profitable signal

An illustrative fast trade earns 3 USDT but reserves the last 100 USDT of collateral. Five minutes later the slow book misses a trade that would have earned 9. Crediting the fast detector +3 is correct trade attribution; enabling it reduced terminal portfolio wealth by 6 in this example. The reverse also happens when the displaced trade loses.

Fork the common account immediately before a contested reservation. One branch accepts the request and one declines it. Keep subsequent market observations and policy rules identical, allow later actions to diverge and run through both settlement and a fixed evaluation horizon. Report own P&L, displaced fills, idle capital-hours and paired terminal wealth. Use a four-way run for the two most frequent competitors: neither, A, B and both. The interaction equals wealth with both minus wealth with A minus wealth with B plus wealth with neither. Use identical starting wealth throughout. A persistently negative interaction calls for a scheduling or sizing experiment before adding another detector.

### 3. Check whether a rare hypothesis can ever be decided on the available horizon

Before spending a holdout, estimate uniquely informative episodes per month **after** correlated listings, outages, infeasible sizes and simultaneous market shocks are accounted for. Simulate the preregistered decision rule using development-period block resamples at the smallest worthwhile net effect and under zero effect. Vary clustering and regime frequency. Report time to a decision, probability of still being inconclusive at the project deadline and false selection frequency. With short history, show sensitivity ranges. Four eligible independent episodes per month and a design requiring sixty already imply roughly fifteen months of accrual before additional data loss. Sixty is illustrative.

Give each candidate an explicit choice: test prospectively, pool economically comparable markets with a transfer test, collect a missing feed, or park it. Start with the existing [holdout ledger](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/holdout_ledger.py). More symbols help only to the extent they add informative episodes.

## Other consequential choices

| Layer | Overlooked decision and concrete experiment | What the result changes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Product economics | Distinguish capital available for risk from a scheduled liability. Start identical TMN-funded paper accounts and require one to meet a fixed TMN withdrawal during a spread or transfer disruption. Measure cash delivered by the deadline, shortfall and post-withdrawal drawdown. Use hypothetical liabilities unless the owner specifies one. | A profitable USDT strategy can be a poor treasury allocation for someone with a local-currency obligation. This determines the relevant benchmark and reserve policy. |
| Data lineage | Preserve both what the process first observed and what the vendor later corrected. Replay decisions against an immutable first-seen snapshot, then score data revisions separately. Inject a corrected candle, out-of-order trade and symbol redenomination; preserve economic units across the last event. | Separates prediction failure from a data correction and prevents a historical improvement from being credited to the model. |
| Universe construction | Treat disappearing instruments as outcomes. Freeze the venue's eligible list at each date, retain names that later halt or delist, and carry their inventory through the actual last executable exit or a range of recovery assumptions. | Measures whether apparent diversification came from retrospectively selecting instruments that survived. |
| Quantitative modeling | Predict the object the trader needs: probability of entry before expiry, expected payoff **conditional on that fill**, and loss if only partially filled. Score and calibrate the components separately. Check fast and slow fills separately because the fast fills may be systematically adverse. | A highly accurate direction model can lose money precisely on the trades it gets. Directs effort to selection, execution or sizing as appropriate. |
| Research controls | Generate sham triggers matched to each real trigger's asset, hour, volatility, spread, holding period and risk budget using only admissible matching variables. Replay them through the same allocator. Add a deliberately time-shifted input as a test that the causal validator catches it. | Shows whether a detector adds information beyond being active in favorable conditions and whether the evaluation would detect leakage. |
| Portfolio risk | Represent common operational exposures alongside return covariance: issuer, stablecoin, venue, collateral asset and transfer rail. Remove one dependency at a time, then impair two together during a broad market shock. Measure executable loss and stranded units. | Two low-correlation strategies can depend on exactly the same exit rail. The result sets limits by shared dependency and can favor redundant inventory. |
| Execution policy | Treat order submission, unknown acknowledgement, late fill and cancel acknowledgement as separate events. Simulate a buy whose acknowledgement times out, whose replacement partially fills and whose original fill arrives late. Reconcile broker-side paper events by order identity. | Defines when a retry, cancel or temporary pause is economically safe. Passing the test means correct net units, cash and fees after every ordering, including restart. |
| Capacity and market impact | Parallel arms can be compared separately as alternatives. Deploying their union requires a shared pool of displayed depth. Test concurrent orders under optimistic and conservative replenishment, and size the same strategy across a capital grid. Record frontier points for net proceeds versus price risk while waiting. | Estimates where scaling or slowing execution changes the best strategy and where evidence runs out. [Existing capacity service](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/strategy_capacity.py) · [Almgren and Chriss](https://doi.org/10.21314/JOR.2001.041) |
| Transfer across markets | Hold out a whole combination of quote currency, venue and liquidity tier. Test whether only the detector transfers, or whether its trigger, sizing and exit all transfer. Include a market with insufficient feed quality as an abstention control. | Supports a common detector with local execution settings when a universal policy fails. |
| Performance engineering | Set a deadline in financial terms for each signal. Replay delays through feature calculation, queuing and routing separately. Rank stages by lost executable opportunity per additional second, then benchmark tail latency under bursty simultaneous alerts. | A slow daily job can be harmless while a cheaper queue delay destroys the short-lived signal. Prioritize using loss attributable to delay alongside CPU load. |
| Operations and recovery | After a recorder gap, compare three restart policies: discard expired entries, recompute targets at current prices, or replay outstanding intentions. Preserve outstanding orders and distinguish entries from protective exits. Exercise a venue reopening with a gap. | Produces a strategy-specific recovery rule and measures the cost of catching up on stale orders. [Existing replay checkpoint](../../gym-core/src/gym/services/replay_checkpoint.py) |
| Operator experience | For a zero-trade week, show eligible market-hours, missing observations, triggers, deduplicated episodes, vetoes, expired intents, capital rejections, submitted orders and fills. Attach one replayable example to each nonempty rejection category. | An operator can distinguish a healthy quiet strategy from broken data, an overactive gate or an untradeable universe before changing thresholds. |
| Research lifecycle | Version the claim as well as the model. Record what observation would retire it, who can inspect the holdout and what change creates a new trial. When a challenger loses, retain its predictions and failure conditions for later comparison. | Stops repeated tuning on a familiar market episode and makes negative results reusable. |

## First delivery

Use the existing ignition book and one challenger. On a frozen development tape emit one row per intent with decision time, proposed units, first eligible execution, data coverage, outcome ambiguity, capital reservation, release and competing intent IDs. Derive the opportunity funnel, ambiguity sensitivity and contested-capital forks from that single output. Run the information-accrual estimate before allocating a prospective window. This slice can reveal whether the next investment should be better data, allocation, execution or a new source of signals.
